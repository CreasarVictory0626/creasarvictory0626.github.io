When one son becomes a monk, seven generations of ancestors ascend to heaven. When a striking success is placed before the public, people scramble to replicate it. Not knowing its core, they copy every detail, for fear of missing anything. When a striking failure appears, people rush to learn from it. Not grasping its root cause, they avoid everything associated with it. The reason is that as "the masses," their essence is defined by limited information. In the face of incomplete knowledge, total replication or total avoidance is indeed a wise strategy. Even so, there are those with sufficient information who look down with condescension, calling them "the mob." And there are others who, after turning failure into success, heap scorn on those who once avoided them. Only a few—those who know the full picture—do not bother to persuade the crowd, but steadfastly hold their ground, quietly toil, and turn failure into success.
